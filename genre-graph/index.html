How to read it

“Never cross the streams — branches split off in the order of the year they happened.”

  • True parentage. A genre branches off its actual parent — thrash off heavy metal, death off thrash — never off a main trunk. Roots are separate starting branches at their own birth years; blues (1920) predates rock, and rock and roll (1949–59) is rock's first era, not a branch.
  • Nested arcs. Branches fan above and below their parent. On each side, the earliest split takes the outermost lane and later splits tuck closer in — a new branch arcs out only as far as its own lane, so no branch ever crosses another.
  • Colour = major genres only. A branch keeps its parent's colour until a major genre is born (23 of them). Everything between majors is one family's shade of the story.
  • Majors carry the weight. The 23 majors — rock, hard rock, heavy metal and the rest — get large bold labels with their band counts, ringed split nodes and full-strength ribbons, so the big trunks read at a glance. Minor branches and small roots (folk, latin, ska…) stay smaller and lighter; their thickness still comes only from their own debut counts.
  • Thickness fluctuates. A branch swells in its busy years and thins in its quiet ones, sized by bands debuting that year — every branch, roots included, on the same scale. A branch in its flood years can run thicker than its parent.
  • Split nodes ride the edge. Each dot sits inside its parent branch's width, toward the edge the child leaves on, so nodes stay visible as the parent swells and thins. Splits sharing a year are nudged apart along the parent so every node stays tappable — the year shown is always the true birth year.
  • Hybrids have two parents in the data. Metalcore (hardcore punk + heavy metal), deathcore, nu metal and friends split from the scene they grew up in; the second parent is recorded in the detail panel, not drawn as a merge — a merge would cross the streams.

Major lane

The narrow lane along the graph's left edge is a quick-jump rail for the 23 major genres — the branches that hold the majority of the bands — ordered by band count, biggest first, each with its birth year and band count. Click one and the graph scrolls to that genre's branch and its detail opens in the panel beside the graph — exactly the same as selecting the branch itself. Rock and roll gets its own button too: it is not a separate branch but rock's first era, so its button jumps to the 1949–1959 stretch of the rock branch and opens rock's panel, with the focus year set inside the era.

Descriptor tags — not genres, not in the lane

Eight tags in the data describe a sound, a format, a shelf or an origin rather than a lineage, so they are not drawn as trees and no longer sit in the lane: vocal (1950 · 17 bands), acoustic (1965 · 12), instrumental (1967 · 5), avantgarde (1979 · 16), african (1984 · 17), world (1995 · 27), experimental (1996 · 7) and holiday (2012 · 7). They remain in the data and in search — find one with “Find a genre” and its description still opens in the side panel.

One knock-on: afrobeats (6 bands, 2011) descends from the descriptor tag “african”, so it is drawn as its own starting branch and its parent is credited here and in its panel.

Data notes & verification

Coverage, honestly: 7,396 of 8,004 bands are placed (92.4%). The 608 unplaced bands have no debut year (415), no resolvable genre (157), a debut year outside 1917–2024 (32), or a junk genre tag (4, e.g. “awesomename”). A band's year is its first album year, with formation year as fallback.

Counting the forest: the data holds 170 genres and 15 roots. Eight genres are descriptor tags — four of them roots (vocal, instrumental, african, world) — and are not drawn in the trees, leaving 162 drawn branches in 12 trees: the 11 non-descriptor roots plus afrobeats, whose only parent is a descriptor. The left lane no longer shows descriptors — it lists the 23 majors (plus rock and roll, rock's first era) as quick jumps.

Long-tail tags (302 of them, 462 bands) count toward the branch genre they fold into, and rock-and-roll-tagged bands count toward rock. Birth years come from the finalized taxonomy: first credible debut year in the data where it is trustworthy, researched overrides where it is not, cross-checked against Musicmap (27 of 35 matched genres agree within 3 years).
