    How to play

    You, Mara, Dev and Kit — seven cards each.

    1. Match the top band. Play a band that shares a musician with the card on top — the connection is named as it lands.
    2. Tap to look, tap PLAY to play. One tap shows a card’s full face, roster and all. The PLAY button sends it to the pile.
    3. Nothing connects? Draw. Take the face-up reveal pile’s top card, one at a time, until a card in your hand connects.
    4. Ricky Vac is wild. The Joker plays on anything — name any band in the deck and he becomes it for the rest of the game.
    5. FREEZE blocks Josh. Play it instead of a band card and the next player can’t connect through Josh Freese on their turn.
    6. First empty hand wins.

    Full rules

    Four seats, seven cards each. The printed rosters decide whether a band move is legal; the two Ricky Vac Jokers can become any band in the deck. First player with no cards left wins.

    1. Deal sevenEach seat starts with seven cards. Flip one band from the deck to begin the chain.
    2. Make the connectionPlay a band that shares at least one musician with the face-up band. The connecting musician is named as the card lands.
    3. Draw until you can moveThe draw pile sits face up. If nothing in your hand connects, take its top card, one at a time, until a playable band is in your hand—then play it.
    4. Keep the chain aliveWhen the reveal pile runs dry, the played stack shuffles back in beneath the top card. If a seat still cannot connect, it passes and the chain moves on.
    5. Play the JokerRicky Vac plays on anything. Name any band in the deck and the Joker becomes that band, including its members, for the rest of the game.
    6. Play FREEZEFREEZE — Josh Freese plays instead of a band card and changes nothing on top. The next player cannot connect through Josh Freese on their turn; a card that also shares another member still plays, and Ricky is never blocked.
    7. Empty your handThe first player to play their final card wins.
    Standard card, no custom shape: every card—including both Jokers—uses the familiar 63 × 88 mm trading-card proportion shared by Pokémon and Magic cards—easy to sleeve, shuffle, store, and print. The playing side follows the main site’s band-card hierarchy: constellation, band, genre · city, years active, then the lineup. This build plays exactly like the printed deck: legality runs on each card’s printed roster — the names on the face, in printed order — and nothing else. Where the full graph holds more members than the card prints, the face notes them as “+N more in the graph”; those names flavor the card but never make a play legal.
